Artemis II lunar flyby

The same mission flown by several models of the physics, scored against NASA's navigation data.

  • The thick neutral line is NASA's track. It is the answer every other line is checked against.
  • Each coloured line is a model. It starts from NASA's position and velocity after the burn to the Moon, gets the same correction burns, and is then on its own.
  • Where a line peels away, that model is missing physics. The scorecard turns the gap into mission terms: flyby altitude, timing, whether Orion gets home.

How far each model drifts

One line per model. The blue cursor is the replay clock; click a chart to move it. Drag across a chart to zoom in time, double-click to zoom back out.

Off-scale rule. Each chart fits its axis to the models whose peak values sit within a factor of 20 of one another, starting from the smallest. A model more than 20 times above that group would flatten the rest, so its line runs off the top of the axis. An arrow marks where it leaves and gives its peak value, the note under the chart names it, and the live table above still shows its value. NASA's line is never clipped. Error charts also have a log scale that shows every model on one axis.

Who can hear Orion

Contact windows with the three Deep Space Network complexes, and the stretch where the Moon blocks the line of sight to Earth. One bar per model; a model that places Orion wrongly predicts the wrong windows.
